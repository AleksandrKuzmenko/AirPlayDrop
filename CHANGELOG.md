# Changelog

## 0.2.0-alpha — 2026-11-18

- Added an Apple-silicon-only DMG packaging pipeline with hardened-runtime signing and Apple notarization.
- Added pinned LGPL FFmpeg 9.0.2 and FFprobe builds with no GPL/nonfree components or network support.
- Prefer bundled media tools while retaining an explicit trusted-directory override for development.
- Removed the unavailable `libx264` fallback in favor of Apple VideoToolbox encoding.
- Fixed media probing failing on files whose FFprobe side data contains numbers (rotated phone videos, HDR10 light-level metadata, Dolby Vision configuration records); the Dolby Vision profile and rotation are now recorded.
- HDR and Dolby Vision files that AVFoundation cannot open (such as MKV) are now re-prepared for Apple TV before AirPlay playback instead of streaming a local-only copy.
- Fixed AV1 videos failing every preparation strategy: the bundled FFmpeg now includes the pinned dav1d 1.5.4 software decoder. With an external FFmpeg that lacks dav1d, AirPlayDrop uses VideoToolbox hardware decoding on Macs that support it and otherwise reports a clear error.
- HDR10 and HLG sources that cannot be stream-copied, such as AV1 and VP9, are now encoded as 10-bit HEVC with explicit BT.2020 and PQ/HLG tags instead of 8-bit H.264. Prepared HDR copies are rejected if they lose their HDR transfer function. The bundled FFmpeg adds the `setparams` filter for this.
- Surround audio is no longer always downmixed to stereo. AAC, AC3, and E-AC3 tracks are copied unchanged, keeping any Dolby Atmos in E-AC3. Other multichannel audio, such as DTS, TrueHD, FLAC, or Opus, is encoded as E-AC3 up to 5.1, with 7.1 downmixed to 5.1. The bundled FFmpeg adds the E-AC3 encoder; builds without it fall back to multichannel AAC. Stereo sources and Late Night mode stay stereo AAC. Copies prepared by earlier versions are re-prepared.
- AirPlay now checks video against what Apple TV can decode, not what the Mac can play. H.264 must be 8-bit 4:2:0 Baseline, Main, or High, HEVC must be 8- or 10-bit 4:2:0 Main or Main 10, and video must be at most 2160p and 60 fps. These limits follow Apple's HLS authoring specification and the Apple TV 4K specifications. Other video, including 10-bit and 4:2:2 H.264, 4:2:2 HEVC, and AV1 or VP9 even in MP4, is converted before AirPlay playback instead of being streamed or stream-copied unchanged. Conversions scale down to 2160p and halve high frame rates when needed, and each AirPlay copy is re-probed and rejected if it is still outside these limits. Locally prepared copies are probed too, so one that is already compatible is not converted again. AirPlay copies prepared by earlier versions are re-prepared. Local playback is unchanged.
- HDR encodes no longer use a fixed 12 Mbit/s. The bit rate follows the HDR HEVC ladder in Apple's HLS authoring specification for the output resolution, such as 7 Mbit/s at 1080p and 20 Mbit/s at 2160p for 30 fps. It is about 20% lower at 24 fps and 50% higher at 60 fps, and never more than 25% above the source's own bit rate when that is known.
- H.264 encodes no longer use a fixed 5 Mbit/s. The bit rate follows the H.264 ladder in Apple's HLS authoring specification for the output resolution, such as 4.5 Mbit/s at 720p and 7.8 Mbit/s at 1080p, scaled by pixel count to 31.2 Mbit/s at 2160p. It is 50% higher at 60 fps, and never more than 125% of the source's own bit rate, or 200% when converting HEVC, VP9, or AV1, which need less bit rate than H.264 for similar quality.
- Dual-layer Dolby Vision profile 7 files, such as UHD Blu-ray rips, now play on Apple TV as their HDR10 base layer. The AirPlay HDR remux removed the Dolby Vision RPU but kept the enhancement layer, which these files carry as NAL type 63 units; it now removes both. The probe records whether the Dolby Vision configuration has an enhancement layer. AirPlay copies prepared under the previous rules are re-prepared.
- Dolby Vision profile 5 files are now kept as Dolby Vision in a `dvh1` MP4 with their configuration box and RPUs, for both AirPlay and local playback. Profile 5 has no HDR10 base layer, so the previous RPU-stripping remux and the HEVC re-encode fallback produced wrong colours; the bundled FFmpeg cannot apply the RPU, so there is no re-encode fallback for profile 5. Prepared copies must still carry the Dolby Vision sample entry and configuration, and copies of profile 5 sources made by earlier versions are re-prepared. AVFoundation now also detects Dolby Vision MP4s from their `dvcC`/`dvvC` configuration box.
- Interlaced video is now deinterlaced. Apple's HLS authoring specification requires it, but encodes of interlaced sources such as broadcast H.264 or MPEG-2 kept the combed fields, and AirPlay stream-copied interlaced H.264 unchanged. Every encode now runs the LGPL `bwdif` deinterlacer at one frame per field, so 25i and 29.97i become 50p and 59.94p, as Apple recommends, and AirPlay converts interlaced video instead of copying it. Local playback still stream-copies interlaced video, which macOS deinterlaces on display. The bundled FFmpeg adds `bwdif`; an external FFmpeg falls back to `yadif`. AirPlay copies prepared under the previous rules are re-prepared.
- Bitmap subtitles (Blu-ray PGS, DVD VobSub, DVB, XSUB) can now be selected and are burned into the video, for both AirPlay and local playback, because MP4 cannot carry them. Choosing one forces a full video encode; text subtitles are still muxed as `mov_text`, and automatic subtitle selection stays text-only. Subtitles are read from a second input of the same file so cues stay frame-accurate on sources with B-frames, are bottom-aligned when a Blu-ray canvas is taller than cropped video, follow the subtitle sync offset, and are dimmed to BT.2408's 203-nit graphics white on HDR video. Dolby Vision profile 5 cannot take burned-in subtitles and reports a clear error. The bundled FFmpeg adds the LGPL `overlay`, `setpts`, and `colorchannelmixer` filters.
- HDR video can now be tone-mapped to SDR. A new Playback Settings choice, **Apple TV HD or SDR-only receiver**, prepares AirPlay video as SDR H.264 up to 1080p, following Apple's Apple TV HD specifications, and tone-maps PQ and HLG to BT.709 with VideoToolbox (`scale_vt`). The default **Apple TV 4K or HDR receiver** keeps HDR as before. Prepared AirPlay copies record their receiver, so changing the setting prepares them again. The local H.264 fallback for HDR sources is tone-mapped too, instead of producing 8-bit video still tagged PQ or HLG. Dolby Vision profile 5 cannot be converted to SDR and reports a clear error. The bundled FFmpeg adds the `scale_vt`, `hwupload`, and `hwdownload` filters.
- Text subtitles can now be burned into the video. Turn on **Burn Into Video** in an item's Subtitles menu to render the selected embedded or sidecar subtitle (SRT, WebVTT, ASS, SSA, mov_text) into the picture instead of muxing it as `mov_text`, keeping ASS styling and fonts. Burn-in follows the subtitle sync offset, dims plain-text subtitles to BT.2408's 203-nit graphics white on HDR video, and is recorded in the prepared copy's manifest. The bundled FFmpeg adds libass 0.17.5 with HarfBuzz 15.0.0, FriBidi 1.0.18, and FreeType 2.15.0, using Core Text for fonts, and the `subtitles` filter; FriBidi's source ships beside the DMG.
- The FFmpeg build script now starts from an empty build directory so it keeps working after Xcode SDK updates.

## 0.1.0-alpha — 2026-09-22

- Replaced positional fallback numbers with capability-driven conversion plans.
- Added deterministic audio and text-subtitle stream selection.
- Added race-safe cancellation, transactional manifests, cache validation, and FFmpeg diagnostics.
- Added explicit local/AirPlay preparation controls with progress, cancel, retry, and cache reuse.
- Added preferred audio and subtitle language settings for newly imported media.
- Added resume playback history and timeline thumbnail previews.
- Added automatic discovery and manual attachment of external text subtitles.
- Added per-item audio and subtitle synchronization controls.
- Added optional Late Night dialogue-focused audio processing.
- Added bounded, cancellable FFprobe execution and AirPlay-safe `hvc1` tagging for HEVC remuxes.
- Added source-release automation, generated-media integration coverage, and open-source project documentation.
